## Recruitment operating strategy

1. Intake: agree commercial outcomes, territory, role scope and evidence standards with the hiring manager. Separate essential from trainable capabilities.
2. Calibrate: compare three fictional profiles across direct and adjacent backgrounds; document disagreements before sourcing at scale.
3. Map: organise company/role hypotheses by sector and market, then validate supply through authorised research and candidate conversations.
4. Engage: use role-specific value propositions and a realistic account of the work. Avoid promises about hiring outcomes or product capabilities.
5. Assess: use consistent questions, practical evidence and human review. Distinguish missing evidence from absent ability.
6. Close: surface candidate motivations and constraints early; coordinate the actual offer process with authorised teams.
7. Learn: inspect stage conversion, ageing and candidate feedback together. A conversion change alone does not identify a cause.

Potential AI hiring challenges include evolving product knowledge, evaluating credible technical-commercial fluency, competition for enterprise sellers and uncertainty around customer use cases. These are strategic hypotheses, not quantified market findings. Broaden sourcing to adjacent sectors while preserving role-specific assessment standards.

## Research limitations

Primary employer/product sources were checked on the research date. Listings may close or change. No external candidate dataset, compensation survey or direct market interviews were available. A real market-sizing exercise requires fresh research and clearly defined geography, role and seniority.

## Competition and adjacent talent

OpenAI's London Account Director posting combines enterprise sales, technical communication, account planning and consumption revenue responsibilities. Anthropic's role examples also combine commercial and technical work. Overlapping competencies suggest competition for some of the same talent. These postings do not reveal candidate preferences, attrition or which employer wins an offer.

AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Accenture and Capgemini are company-level research hypotheses. They are not verified available candidate pools. Cloud organisations can also be distribution partners, so a source organisation need not be a product competitor.

| Background | Potential transfer | Evidence to challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise SaaS | Buying-group navigation and procurement | Personal ownership of a complex sale rather than renewal administration |
| Cloud platforms | Platform adoption and consumption | Ability to connect technical usage with measurable customer value |
| Cybersecurity | Risk stakeholders and technical evaluation | Ability to learn different use cases without overstating product assurance |
| Consulting | Discovery and transformation | Personal quota ownership where the vacancy requires selling responsibility |

The UK DSIT AI Labour Market Survey 2025 executive summary, published January 2026, reports non-technical skills gaps among 30% of surveyed businesses. This is UK AI-sector context, not an estimate of GTM talent shortages or EMEA candidate supply. The survey is self-selecting and needs role-level evidence before supporting a search decision.

## Sourcing experiments

Start with an agreed scorecard. Compare direct AI experience with adjacent-sector examples against the same essential capabilities. Use authorised professional tools, voluntary referrals and relevant communities. Track response, screen quality, progression and candidate feedback separately. Scale a source after hiring-manager calibration rather than response volume alone. Expand geography only when business need and hiring arrangements support it.

For each market-map entry, record employer, role family, territory, relevance rationale, public source URL, check date and next validation step. Candidate-level research requires separate authority; this demonstration holds fictional records only.
